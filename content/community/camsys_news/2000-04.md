{{begin-newsitem |time=2000-04-19T12:40:12Z |title=Diablo 2 Beta Report |author=King Arthur |site=Camsys |categories=Camsys}}
Well I'm at school now and am unfortunately unable to play any Diablo 2 at the moment, so I thought I'd share with you my thoughts on the Beta, specifically dealing with the different character classes for now since a lot of people have asked about this particular topic.

Since I gave a testimonial for the paladin and the barbarian, and since you wanted to hear about the other three classes, here goes:

**Amazon**: I like a "javazon" the best, i.e. an amazon that uses javelins/spears/etc rather than the bows and such. I guess cause it still feels like I'm in the midst of combat, rather than sniping from far away. Not that sniping is bad (it's great in Unreal Tournament for instance), but there's not as much fun when "sniping" in D2 cause you feel like you're out of the action. She's got a really good skill tree, allowing you to tailor your amazon to be either a really good bow or spear warrior. Or a combination of both, able to switch when the situation calls for it.

**Barbarian**: Hands down this guy has the best weapon capabilities of any character in the game. And this is really appropriate for this type of character, because he's going to be doing almost exclusively melee combat (unless for some reason you want your barbarian to be a wussy bowman). The advantages given by the ability to weild two weapons at once are incredible. Sure you don't have a shield, but with two weapons at hand, and especially with the Double Swing skill, you'll hardly give any monsters a chance to get a good hit. Bash is also a great skill, as it adds quite a bit of damage and accuracy to your attacks, for only a few mana. Originally I thought I wouldn't be putting hardly any stat points into Energy, because I figured well he has no spells. Well, believe me it's worth it. Most of his good skills do use mana, especially the combat related ones (like Bash). The only thing that prevents the barbarian for being the best character in the game (in my opinion that is) is his warcries. In practice they don't seem to be very useful. A few of them act similar to the paladin's auras, but in reality you're better off having the default attack as bash, and the secondary as double swing (or visa versa). Hotkeying a warcry might be ok, but I doubt that you'll find the need to use them, especially when you get past Act 1. I don't think I'd get rid of them, however, because they do seem very fitting for his character, after all. But perhaps not have quite so many of them? :) Another skill that might be useful for him is the ability to "Sharpen" or "Repair" on his own, possibly like the Warrior could do in D1. I say this because it seems logical (warriors did sharpen and repair their own weapons when needed), and running back to Charsi or a later equivalent does get tedious after a while. Other than that the Barbarian is an excellent character for anyone that likes melee fighting. The only thing you have to worry about is healing potions. The paladin doesn't really have that much of a problem (thanks to Prayer), but the Barbarian definately does (unless you can get one weapon that steals life, and another that steals mana - which I have done and has proved very useful).

**Necromancer**: This guy can be fun, but only at higher character levels. I like the fact that he almost always has a big posse around him, but again this is sort of like the amazon, it can begin to feel like it leaves you with nothing to do, especially if you have a paladin healing your minions (i.e. meaning you don't have to spawn more). The so called "Norman Bates" attack is a riot for low level necros, but it gets old real fast. I've picked up a few REALLY good Necro wands (that you can buy at 25000+ and sell at 5000+ - most expensive in the game), and so typically those do much better (if you can find them). Most of the good ones boost the Necro's skills a lot, and with the Necro that's very important because he's not a real physical type of fighter. If you find yourself relying more on physical attacks, or trying to pump up your necro, perhaps you chose the wrong character class. That's not to say you can't play him that way, but he's not really meant for that. Although in a pinch it can be nice to be able to hack and slash almost as well as the paladin or barbarian (even though you'll be limited to what weapons you can use).

**Paladin**: Not your traditional hack and slash warrior. The barbarian actually fills that role much better, in my opinion, since he typically gets better attack and defense ratings, as well as damage. Don't get me wrong though, that's not to say that the Paladin is a pansy. Far from it, in fact. His auras make him in my opinion the best character class in the game, especially in team combat situations. Having a paladin around to heal you as you duke it out with the really high level monsters (Andariel for example) is incredibly useful. One might say necessary, even. The paladin requires a bit more "hands on" control than the Barbarian, however. I'd suggest the barbarian as a starting character for traditional melee characters, and then moving up to the paladin for some cool (and useful) differences. One of the keys to being a successful paladin is properly managing your auras. That is to say, knowing when each aura is useful, and being able to quickly switch between them. Having this ability can literally mean the salvation of either yourself or your party. The barbarian doesn't have to do a lot of skill changing (which is why I think he's easier to play), but the paladin must in order to be effective. One thing that I didn't realize until too late was how effective a skill Sacrifice and Zeal are. In fact most of his Combat skill tree is much more effective than one might think at first. Certainly his auras make him nearly immortal, Prayer in particular. However I've found that his combat skills have saved his life time and again as well, particularly when you get around to facing the high level monsters like the Banished, the Afflicted, and the Arachs. They are especially useful when facing unique monsters and their minions or champions (who are usually in packs of 3 each). Dumping skill points into Prayer early on is a must for any paladin who doesn't want to be constantly knocking back potions. That also means that you need to invest at least one to two skill points per level-up into Energy, in order to provide Prayer with plenty of Mana to restore your health with. Thankfully Prayer is the only aura (I think) that uses Mana, so if you're out of mana, you should switch to an alternative aura (such as Defiance or Might - both of which will help to clear out a room faster now that your Healing rate is slowing down). This last aspect is important, as it gives your mana a chance to recharge, and helps you to make sure that you're not facing deadly fireballs or those nasty lightning balls the Afflicteds shoot at you for longer than you should be. Plus boosting your Attack rating to some ungodly number that usually only the Barbarian is capable of is quite useful as well :) In short, the Paladin is my favorite character in the game, probably because of his vast capabilities, his usefulness in team play, and how well he fits my personality :)

**Sorceress**: I think I've played her the least of any of the classes. I was going to say aside from the amazon, but I just remembered that I played the amazon quite a bit actually last summer. Not as much as the paladin but enough so that I got to see her look cute in plate mail as she made it to act 3. In any case, the Sorceress, of course, has one of the most fun skill trees of any of the characters. Except for maybe the Necromancer, all the other skill trees seem pretty grim, or at least so concentrated on combat that you lose out on some cool effects, etc. I guess what I'm trying to say is that the Sorceress has some of the coolest spells and effects of any of the other characters. She's able to specialize in Cold, Fire, and Lightning skills (as far as I can remember), which means of course that you can make a jack of all trades type character or a specialized sorceress on par with Merlin et al.

The whole thing comes down to what character class fits your personality best. All of the character classes rock. Initially I was quite depressed that playing as a paladin, I wouldn't be able to raise golems or cast really cool spells, etc. But the specialization actually works better. Instead of everyone being a carbon copy of everyone else (albiet with different stats) ala Diablo 1, you can now have really cool team games where you augment each other's skills and end up doing a much better job of fending off the baddies. If one monster is really giving you a hard time, then I'm sure it will be cake for a character from another class to finish it off for you. And visa versa of course. Even players of the same character class can still find room enough so that they can all be specializing in different areas and still work well together as a team (the amazon and the sorceress are the best examples of this). The rule of thumb should always be to play with a paladin, however. His auras provide all players on a team with noticable and invaluable effects, which often will save you from certain death at the hands of a large pack of high level monsters (as happens when your character gets up around level 15+). So even if you're not as enthusiastic about the paladins as I am, treat the ones you find with respect, and never take their auras for granted. Beleive me, you'll miss them dearly when they're gone.

Anyways, that's my report as of right now. I've got a few really kick-ass screenshots that I got last night in a battle with a friend of mine, as well as a few solo ones. I'll hopefully be posting them later for you to take a peek at.

Unfortunately, I have decided to stop work on my action script compiler.
