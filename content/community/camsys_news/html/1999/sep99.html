Programming Tutorial Update - - 00:24 EST
  • It has come to my attention recently that a large number of you have become very interested in the Programming Tutorials that I have created so far. When I set out I intended to create 21-days' worth of tutorials that would eventually teach you enough so that I felt that when you finished, you'd have just about the same amount of knowledge as I did when creating them. Many of you have told me that you eagerly await the rest of the tutorials, and I am pleased to hear that, just as I was pleased to hear so many of you were reading them at all.
    I have every intention of completing this tutorial series. I think that right now it's one of the most important tasks that I have set before me... In my opinion, although you can learn much of what the tutorials have to offer by majoring in Computer Science, I believe that most Computer Science programs are funamentally flawed, in that the software engineers the curriculum produces are ill equipped to tackle real-world problems. The reason seems to be, from observing my classmates, that they did not spend any great length of time on non-class related programming. It seemed that they all lacked a creative spark to propell their quest for knowledge, and to expand their abilities as a programmer. I think the Computer Science courses that they take have stripped away the magical aspect of programming, and its ultimate results, so that the end result is someone with a degree which doesn't really mean anything.
    My goal with these tutorials is to try and provide as many of you with that creative spark as I can. Reading what I've written, doing the examples, etc is not enough in and of itself. You have to seek out projects to focus your knowledge on and produce something unique and challenging.
    Well in any case, I did have the thought last night of re-structuring and re-ordering the tutorials, and removing the 21-day restriction. I think that such a restriction is artificial, and to imply that you can learn all that I have set forth in 21 days is absurd. Removing that restriction also enables me to add topics as I see fit and to re-arrange the existing topics should I find that one sequence is more successful than the rest. I also intend to compile a reference that you can turn to of examples of many of the things I have illustrated in the tutorials. Now that I have an idea of just how many people are interested in these tutorials, I feel that what I have already done is inadequate and below the standards I would have set if I had thought that there was to be such a wide-spread following for them. So I may try and rewrite the existing tutorials before I continue on. I will probably keep both versions up in case one format seems easier to understand than the other.
    In any case, I thank you all for your feedback on the tutorials, I wanted to let you know where I was going with them, and to let you know that I am going to keep working on them, as soon as I am able to. I may start rewriting tomorrow if I have time.
